Surgery Intern Guide

🔎
Handbook-based reference. This reorganizes the uploaded Surgery Guide for faster bedside use. It is not an independent clinical protocol. For medication doses, anticoagulation, transfusion, sepsis, emergencies, or any conflict with current policy/orders, verify with your senior/attending and current hospital policy.

Today / Daily Workflow

6 quick-reference cards

Before 6 AM⌄

Arrive early enough to pre-round before 6 AM. Review overnight events, vitals, I/O, drains/tubes (8-hour + 24-hour output and character), procedure/POD, and draft notes before rounds. Strip drains when appropriate. Early intern notes should be reviewed by the PGY-2 buddy before signing.

6:00 AM — Rounds⌄

Be ready in white coat at the 2nd-floor elevators at 6:00 AM. Floor patients are presented by the intern/medical student; ICU/new patients by PGY-2 or overnight consult resident. Keep door presentations concise. Rounds should finish by 7:00 AM.

7:00 AM — Morning Report⌄

Monday–Friday in the boardroom. Overnight resident gives brief table sign-out; consult resident presents new consults; overnight intern reviews/presents relevant imaging.

During the day⌄

Keep the patient list and OR list updated. Re-round on patients. Complete pre-ops, follow labs/results, prepare discharges, perform/coordinate dressing changes, and communicate changes.

Afternoon sign-out⌄

Usually 2–4 PM. Know acute events, current vitals, outputs, plan, new consults, and pending tasks. Sign-out is not automatic dismissal; finish floor work and check out with the on-call resident/chief before leaving.

Night intern⌄

Know all patients, complete late postop checks, ensure pre-op patients are booked/consented/cleared with needed orders, order appropriate morning labs, update the list, and have the OR schedule/add-ons plus current vitals/labs ready.

Pre-op Checklist

6 quick-reference cards

Core checklist⌄

1) Book case. 2) Review required labs. 3) Determine if EKG/CXR are indicated. 4) Consent. 5) Medical ± specialty risk stratification. 6) Review anticoagulant/antiplatelet plan. 7) Determine blood-product needs. 8) Place midnight pre-op orders. 9) Check special orders/cultures. 10) NPO sign. 11) Working IV.

Booking⌄

Handbook: submit booking sheet to OR receptionist before noon; overnight/weekend/holiday booking goes through nursing manager when OR is closed.

Labs⌄

Handbook uses ≤3 days for CBC/CMP or BMP ± Mg/PO4/coags and a 72-hour hard limit for Type & Screen. It contains additional blood-bank workflow details—review the full section when ordering.

Consent⌄

Obtain procedural and blood-transfusion consent as applicable; stickers on both sides; write procedure fully without abbreviations; nurse co-witness. If unsure of wording, ask the senior.

Midnight orders⌄

Handbook lists NPO, discontinue diet, IV fluids, and anticoagulation adjustments as procedure-specific pre-op considerations. Discuss orders with the primary/senior team, especially ICU/CCU patients.

Final bedside check⌄

Confirm NPO status/sign and a functioning IV. Follow up every order you place.

Post-op / Discharge

6 quick-reference cards

Post-op check⌄

Approximately 4 hours postop. Document POD 0, acute events, vitals, I/O/urine output, focused cardio/resp/abdominal exam, dressing, and plan.

Post-op plan prompts⌄

Pain • bowel regimen • diet • antibiotics • IVF • prophylaxis (SCD/IS/PPI as applicable) • anticoagulation • strict I/O • labs • consults • special precautions/equipment.

Transfer from OR⌄

Before using the transfer function, place needed postop orders first; review each carried-forward order for necessity.

Ready for discharge⌄

Handbook checklist: tolerating PO, pain controlled on PO meds, voiding, ambulating, home meds addressed, home care/rehab arranged, prescriptions sent, follow-up organized, discharge paperwork complete.

Admitted-service discharge⌄

Sequence in handbook: discharge medications → discharge plan/hospital course → Adult Transfer & Discharge Summary → discharge order.

AMSU discharge⌄

Confirm procedure, activity, pain regimen, equipment, dressing instructions, anticoagulation, diet, and follow-up with the attending.

Floor Calls

8 quick-reference cards

First move⌄

Go see the patient. Recheck vitals. Review the chart/trends. Perform a focused exam. Identify whether the patient is unstable. Escalate early to the senior/on-call resident for concerning changes.

Fever⌄

Recheck temperature and evaluate the patient. The handbook includes a postoperative fever framework and targeted workup guidance; use the patient’s POD, symptoms, exam, lines/drains and current hospital policy to guide workup with the senior.

Low urine output⌄

Check I/O and trends, Foley patency if present, bladder scan for obstruction, BP/MAP, volume status and renal function. Be cautious with fluids/diuresis in CHF/ESRD and discuss treatment with the senior.

Hypotension⌄

Assess mental status, MAP/HR/UOP, bleeding vs volume loss and operative sites; obtain IV access and escalate urgently. The handbook specifically emphasizes comorbid CHF/ESRD and higher level of care when not responding.

Bleeding⌄

Evaluate immediately and apply direct pressure when appropriate. Check operative site/compartments and escalate. Persistent or expanding bleeding can represent a surgical emergency.

Tachycardia / SOB⌄

Assess pain, volume status, oxygenation, respiratory effort and other causes. Consider monitoring/EKG/workup based on presentation and involve the senior early.

Nausea / vomiting⌄

Assess cause, abdominal exam, diet/PO status, bowel function and procedure. The handbook contains medication suggestions; verify current orders, contraindications and senior preference before treatment.

Pain⌄

Confirm diagnosis and existing regimen, use multimodal/stratified pain control as appropriate, check hold parameters, and remember bowel regimen with opioids.

Presentations & Notes

5 quick-reference cards

New patient presentation⌄

Age/sex → chief complaint → brief HPI/ROS → PMH/PSH/meds/allergies → vitals → physical exam including lines/tubes/outputs → labs/imaging → assessment & plan.

Hospitalized patient⌄

Age/sex → diagnosis/procedure + POD/HD → overnight events → vitals including Tmax/O2/I&O/mental status → pain/ambulation/voiding/diet → resulted labs/studies → assessment → today’s plan.

Progress notes⌄

Draft before 6 AM. Every patient gets a daily progress note unless starred or on the follow-up/line list. Acute surgical events still require documentation.

Door presentation⌄

Intern/medical student: floor patients. PGY-2/overnight consult resident: ICU/new patients. Intern remains responsible for knowing the patient.

Post-op note⌄

Use abbreviated SOAP format with procedure/POD 0, events, vitals, I/O, focused exam/dressing and a complete postoperative plan.

On Call / AMSU

4 quick-reference cards

AMSU H&P⌄

If on call the next day, handbook assigns AMSU H&P/consent coverage; give AMSU your pager when they open around 06:45–07:00. Monday call coverage is handled Friday.

OR / office handoff⌄

Sign out to a covering intern/resident before leaving the floor; hand off the pager when going to the OR. If no intern is available, sign out to the on-call resident.

Weekend / holiday⌄

Sign-out is 7:00 AM sharp in the charting room, followed by walking rounds. Note-writing responsibilities are shared as described in the handbook.

Night checklist⌄

Late postop checks • all patients reviewed • pre-ops ready • morning labs appropriate • list updated • OR schedule/add-ons ready • current vitals/labs ready for sign-out.

Policies / Schedule

6 quick-reference cards

Dress code⌄

Professional attire for Friday education and clinic/office days with clean white coat. White coat over scrubs outside OR/charting room. Eye protection required in OR. No lanyards; ID visible above waist.

Sick day⌄

Submit on date of absence and text chief resident as soon as possible; handbook also asks notification of chief/coordinator preferably about an hour before morning sign-out when possible.

Personal day⌄

Submit at least 2 weeks in advance.

Holiday makeup⌄

Use within 30 days of holiday worked; request at least 2 weeks in advance.

Other schedule requests⌄

Submit by the 1st of the preceding month. Post-publication swaps are voluntary, within the appropriate call pool/rotation site, and must not create an ACGME violation.

Professionalism⌄

Keep communication professional; address concerns with the person involved before escalating when appropriate; personal concerns should not be placed in the residency group chat.

Pocket Guide

4 quick-reference cards

Carry⌄

Stethoscope • pen light • tape • gauze/ABD • trauma shears/scissors/suture-removal tools • long Q-tips/tongue depressor • bacitracin • saline flushes • alcohol swabs • Adaptic/iodoform • Kerlix • Surgilube.

Before rounds⌄

Patient list • pen • vitals/I&O • drain/tube outputs • labs • imaging • overnight events • procedure/POD • dressing supplies when needed.

Before leaving⌄

Re-round • follow results • finish pre-ops • complete discharge work • update list • communicate outstanding tasks • sign out • check with on-call resident/chief.

Golden rule⌄

If you do not know, do not guess. Ask your senior.
